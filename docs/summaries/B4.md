# Phase B4 summary: the device channel and online fueling

## What was done

### Schema (`V7__devices_agents_fuelings.sql`, `V7_1__fueling_hardening.sql`)

- `pos_devices` gains the device's public key (EC P-256) and a check that an active device has one.
- New tables:
  - `device_nonces`: the replay guard for token requests.
  - `agent_sessions`: hashed tokens, one live session per agent.
  - `fuelings`: one row per decided attempt, never updated, with the receipt snapshot (plate, company, station, agent).
  - `alerts`.
- `fueling_reference_seq` gives receipt references `F-10001`.
- Row-level security:
  - `device_nonces` and `agent_sessions` are platform only.
  - A company reads its own fuelings and an operator reads those at its stations; only the platform writes them.
  - A company sees only the alerts meant for it.
- V7_1 makes a fueling row immutable for every role, the owner included, and checks that its company, operator, station, device and agent agree.

### Device channel (`network`)

- **Activation:** a one-time code (15 minutes, 5 tries) binds the terminal's own key.
- **Device tokens:** they last 15 minutes and are issued for a signed timestamp and nonce. They are signed with a key derived under its own label, so a device token and a user token never stand in for each other.
- **Agent sign-in:**
  - Worker ID and PIN, hashed with Argon2id; 5 wrong PINs lock the agent for 15 minutes.
  - A forced PIN change at first sign-in. A wrong current PIN during the change counts towards the same lockout.
  - One live session per agent, at the agent's own station only.
  - Sessions end on suspension, PIN reset or lockout.
- **A separate security chain for `/api/v1/pos/**`:**
  - It re-reads the device, its operator and the agent session on every request.
  - A refusal says what to do next: `AGENT_SESSION_REQUIRED` or `PIN_CHANGE_REQUIRED`.

### Fueling engine (`fueling`)

- **Locks, always in this order:** the wallet, then the vehicle (shared), then the tag. The facts are read under those locks, and `FuelingRules` decides in PRD order.
- **The balance is decided before anything is written,** so the ledger never refuses.
- **Idempotent on `clientTxnId`,** under a race too.
- **On approval:** the cooldown starts, and hard alerts are raised in the same transaction (tank capacity; VIP ceiling, for Ops only; low balance).
- **Receipts:** a signed link that needs no sign-in.
- **Reads:**
  - Lists and details for Ops, the company and the station.
  - Alerts with acknowledge, under the new `ALERTS_MANAGE` permission for Super Admin and Ops.
- **VIP is switched off** (`zawed.fueling.vip-enabled=false`) until the owner decides who pays for VIP fuel. While off, a VIP vehicle fuels and is billed like a normal one.
- **Scope escalations are a closed list.** Every place that runs as the platform is named, and `ArchitectureTests` fails on a new one.

### Web

- **Live POS (`VITE_API_MODE=http`):**
  - The browser activates as a terminal. Its private key is non-extractable and stays in IndexedDB.
  - Device tokens are signed on demand. WebCrypto's r‖s signature is converted to DER, checked against Node's verifier.
  - Agent sign-in, PIN change, a simulated tag read and volume entry.
  - A retry reuses the `clientTxnId`, so a fueling is never charged twice.
  - The result shows the decline reason or the thermal receipt, the wallet as a status word only, and a QR to the public receipt page `/receipts/:id`.
- **Portal screens:** fuelings lists and details for Ops, the company and the station, with status and Cairo-day filters. Alerts for Ops (with acknowledge) and for the company. Both languages.

### Fable security review (2026-11-13)

The review found no double charge, no overdraft race, no posting without its fueling row, no cross-tenant read and no token confusion. Both B3 findings this phase was meant to close are closed: the ledger posting is never a refusal path, and there is one named escalation instead of wider policies.

| # | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **High:** suspending an operator did not stop its devices | **Fixed:** the device check and the station's open state require an active operator; test added |
| 2 | **High:** VIP was live; it fuelled with no limits and no posting, so the station was owed money the ledger did not show | **Fixed:** switched off by default (plan §1); the switched-on flow is tested separately. *Owner decision still open: who pays for VIP fuel.* |
| 3 | Medium: a deadlock between a fueling and "report tag stolen" (opposite lock order) | **Fixed:** the engine locks the vehicle before the tag; a deadlock victim is retried once |
| 4 | Medium: PIN change was a PIN oracle outside the lockout | **Fixed:** it counts towards the lockout, the lock ends the sessions, and it has its own rate limit |
| 5 | Medium: anyone who knew a device id could starve that device of tokens | **Fixed:** limited by address before the proof and per device after it |
| 6 | Low: sessions survived suspension and PIN reset | **Fixed** |
| 7 | Low: a ledger refusal would have shown the balance to the POS | **Fixed:** converted to a server error, and the tag's company is asserted against the wallet's |
| 8 | Low: tests that did not cover their claim | **Fixed:** duplicate-key branch, same-tag race, 50 in parallel, parallel sign-ins and wrong PINs; the escalation scan catches any mention of `runAs` |

## Not in B4

- **Offline fuelings and sync (B5).** The `origin` column is ready, and the B5 conflict policy is still an owner decision.
- **No pre-check before dispensing.** The POS records a fueling after the volume is entered, so a decline reaches the agent before printing but after the pump. A pre-authorisation call is a B5 or hardware-spike question.
- **The tag UID is typed into the simulator.** A real reader (NFC, behind a `TagReader`) belongs to the Kotlin POS.
